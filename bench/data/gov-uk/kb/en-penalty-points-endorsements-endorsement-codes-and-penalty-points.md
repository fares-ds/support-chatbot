# Penalty points (endorsements)

# Endorsement codes and penalty points

Each endorsement has a special code and is given ‘penalty points’ on a scale from 1 to 11. You get more points for more serious offences.

The table shows the offence codes that can be put on your driving record. It also shows how many penalty points you can get for them. Some offences may also involve a disqualification.

Offence codes and penalty points must stay on your driving record for 4 or 11 years depending on the offence.

## Offences without penalty points

A non-endorsable offence is a motoring offence where you’re disqualified from driving, but you do not get any penalty points.

If you get no penalty points but are disqualified from driving for 56 or more days, you will have to pay to renew your licence. You can only renew your licence after the disqualification period ends.

## Special code

Code TT99 must stay on a driving record for 4 years from the date of conviction.

It shows disqualification under ‘totting-up’ - if the total of penalty points reaches 12 or more within 3 years, the driver can be disqualified.

## ‘Mutual recognition’ codes

You’ll get a mutual recognition code, known as an ‘MR’ code if you’re disqualified while driving in the Republic of Ireland.

MR codes are added to your UK driving record. The disqualification is also valid in Great Britain and the code stays on the record for 4 years from the date of conviction.

DVLA records these offence codes on the drivers’ database so that you and third parties (for example, insurance companies and the police) can view the information online.

If you are disqualified while driving in Northern Ireland or the Isle of Man, you’ll get an NE99 offence code on your driving licence.

## Aiding, abetting, counselling or procuring offences

If you help someone else to commit an offence this means you may have ‘aided, abetted, counselled or procured an offence’. You’ll get the same points or disqualifications as you would have if you had committed the offence yourself.

For these offences, the codes are similar, but with the number 0 on the code changed to 2.

For example, code LC20 (driving otherwise than in accordance with a licence) becomes code LC22 on your driving record if you have helped someone to do this.

## Causing or allowing someone to commit an offence

For these offences, the codes are similar, but with number 0 changed to 4.

For example, LC20 (driving otherwise than in accordance with a licence) becomes LC24 on your licence if you’ve caused or permitted someone to do this.

## Encouraging or persuading someone to commit an offence

The codes for these offences are set out in the above endorsement codes and penalty point section, but with number 0 on the code changed to 6. For example, DD40 (dangerous driving) becomes DD46 on your driving record if you’ve encouraged or persuaded someone to do this.

The penalty points for the conviction are the same.
